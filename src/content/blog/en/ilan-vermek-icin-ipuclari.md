A well-written listing is the first step toward your plot reaching its true price.
The seven tips below are simple rules that help both private sellers and offices
get more messages on arsam.net.

## 1. Shoot in daylight, keep it landscape

Landscape photos taken on a cloudy afternoon convey the plot's boundary and gradient
most accurately. If you have a drone, an aerial shot from the north adds credibility
to your listing.

## 2. Put location + size in the title

A title like "Road-facing 1,250 m² residential plot in Alaçatı, Çeşme" gets three to
four times more clicks than "Beautiful plot for sale". Putting filterable keywords —
region, m², zoning — in the title improves both SEO and in-search ranking.

## 3. Benchmark against regional average price

Check the average TL/m² on the region page and place your listing within ±15% — that
usually delivers a balanced outcome. The "Pricing help" panel shows a 12-month price
trend; the listing wizard reminds you of the gap before you publish.

## 4. Pick zoning and deed correctly

Saying "agricultural" instead of "unzoned", or "shared" instead of "freehold deed",
helps the right buyer find your plot at the right time. The wrong category produces
noise and disappointment.

## 5. Confirm the KVKK consent box

The final step of the wizard is the KVKK privacy consent. Without that box ticked
the listing does not go live; arsam.net forwards inquiries only from consented
listings.

## 6. Mark location by neighbourhood / village

You may not want to share exact parcel coordinates — fair. Even so, "Reşadiye
Neighbourhood, Datça" is more useful than just "Datça centre" so the map pin lands
close to reality.

## 7. Mention surroundings in the description

Concrete details like "1.2 km to the coast", "well water available" or "15 years in
the same family" produce more than half of total messages. It's not flourish — it's
verifiable specifics that convert.

The wizard surfaces these seven items as a checklist. If a field is incomplete you'll
see a warning before publishing, so there's no risk.
