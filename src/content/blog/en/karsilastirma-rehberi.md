Picking the "right" plot from two or three options in the same region is the most
critical step facing an informed buyer. The [/karsilastir](/karsilastir) page on
arsam.net turns that step into a table — but the questions you bring to it determine
the answers you get out.

## Which rows matter most?

The compare table has more than 12 fields, but decisions usually land on these five:

1. **TL/m² and gap to regional average.** A listing that is 10% cheaper or pricier
   than the regional average is either a genuine deal or hides a flaw — the other
   rows explain which.
2. **Zoning status.** A 200% price gap between a residential 800 m² plot and an
   agricultural one of the same size is not a surprise; honest comparison happens
   "apples to apples".
3. **Deed type.** Freehold deeds make sales smoother. Shared deeds may require co-
   owner consent and lengthen the process — which is why prices can run 20-40% lower.
4. **Road frontage and width.** A plot without frontage typically implies right-of-
   way trouble. The ratio of frontage to width tells you how much of the plot is
   actually usable.
5. **Utility access.** Electricity, water and especially natural-gas connections
   explain a meaningful share of the TL/m² delta.

## Using the compare bar

Each search-result card carries a "Compare" checkbox. The selections live in your
browser's local storage — no account needed. Tap the floating bar at the bottom-right
or visit `/karsilastir` directly to see the table.

## How many listings at once?

For now, **up to 4 listings** can be compared simultaneously. We picked that ceiling
so the table remains legible on a phone. If your list is full when you try to add a
fifth, you'll see a warning; remove an existing row by clicking its trailing X.

## Sharing the URL

The compare-page URL carries the listing IDs as parameters. Copy and send it to a
partner and they open the same table — a handy feature for buying teams.

The compare table is the bridge between desk research and an office visit. Knowing
which row drives the decision in advance makes the meeting itself easier.
