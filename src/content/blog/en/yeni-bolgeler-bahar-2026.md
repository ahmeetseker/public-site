Spring 2026 marks a turning point in the land market: buyer attention is shifting from
seaside residential plots to agricultural parcels. Below, a short portrait of four
regions drawn from arsam.net's last 6 months of active listings. The full methodology
is explained at [/yardim/karsilastirma-rehberi](/yardim/karsilastirma-rehberi).

## Çeşme — Alaçatı and Ovacık

Average TL/m² runs 12% above last season, yet the number of new listings is up 30% —
a sign that supply is strengthening alongside prices. Residential-zoned plots still
lead, particularly north of Alaçatı where 800-1,500 m² parcels cluster.

## Bodrum — Yalıkavak, Gümüşlük

In Yalıkavak's luxury belt residential prices have stabilised; the activity has moved
to Gümüşlük. Here the share of shared-deed plots is above the regional average — the
negotiation window is wider for buyers, but the process can drag.

## Datça — Reşadiye, Yazı

The Datça peninsula stands out as the season's new magnet. 60% of new listings here
are agricultural-zoned or vineyard/orchard parcels; the average plot size is around
2,500 m². The 8% year-over-year price rise sits below the regional average — but
infrastructure access is limited and road frontage matters.

## Kaş — Kalkan, Çukurbağ Peninsula

Kaş remains one of the few coasts where listings can offer direct sea frontage. On
the Çukurbağ Peninsula the average TL/m² is 18% above last year while the listing
count is flat. That's classic demand-outpacing-supply behaviour; expect narrow
negotiation margins.

## Headlines

- **Agricultural plots are climbing.** Buyers seeking seaside-alternative are turning
  to Datça, the Marmaris hinterland and Kaş back-country.
- **Shared-deed share varies by region.** High in Bodrum and Datça; low in Çeşme and
  Kaş.
- **Utilities explain the price gap.** Plots with electricity + water carry a 25%
  premium versus the regional average TL/m².
